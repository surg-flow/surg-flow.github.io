Abstract

DRAFT  Replace with the final camera-ready abstract.

Manipulation intent has two parts: where to act on an object, and how that object should move. We represent both as contact-coupled flow — a dense object-centric 3D flow field paired with a probabilistic contact field defined over the same query points. Unlike tool trajectories or robot joint actions, this pair is embodiment-invariant: it describes the task rather than the arm that performs it.

We extract contact-coupled flow from action-free surgical video using a training-free estimator that combines motion coupling, engagement gating and distance shading over tracked query points. It uses no force sensing and no proprioception, and is grounded per embodiment by two landmarks only — forceps jaws for a surgical robot, thumb and index finger for a human demonstrator.

A generator predicts future contact-coupled flow from a single point-cloud observation, and a model-based executor follows it: it grasps at the contact maximum and servos along the predicted flow with receding-horizon re-prediction, monitoring its own contact belief to detect slip and recover from it. We evaluate tissue retraction on a da Vinci Research Kit and on a humanoid laparoscopic platform, including transfer from human video alone.

Method

From action-free video to a robot-executable representation.

Training-free contact extraction

Contact is inferred from motion coupling — how strongly a tissue point moves with the instrument — gated by an engagement test so that nothing is attributed to contact before the tool actually interacts, and shaded by distance so that nearby evidence counts more than far evidence. The estimator is physically normalized rather than per-episode percentile-normalized, so contact probabilities mean the same thing across episodes.

Embodiment-invariant by construction

Because both channels live on the object, a human demonstration and a robot demonstration of the same task produce the same target representation. Grounding requires only a two-landmark correspondence between the demonstrator's end effector and the robot's.

Execution with contact monitoring

The executor grasps at the contact maximum and follows the predicted flow. Because the contact estimator runs online, the same signal that trained the representation also serves as a slip detector at test time, triggering regrasp when the contact belief collapses.
