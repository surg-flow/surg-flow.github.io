Abstract

Paired video–action demonstrations enable autonomous surgical behavior, but such data is scarce by construction: robots perform roughly 1% of the world's surgeries, while video-only data is orders of magnitude more abundant. Learning 3D object flow offers an embodiment-agnostic way to utilize the abundant video data, but flow alone specifies how an object should move, not where and when the tool should engage it, a distinction that is critical in surgery.

We introduce SurgFlow, a framework that learns 3D Object-Centric Contact Flow from stereo surgical video without robot action labels. For each object point, the representation pairs a future 3D trajectory with a contact score, jointly encoding object motion and the tool contact required to produce it. We extract these targets from video by 3D tracking and tool–object proximity, train a flow matching generator to predict them, and control different robot platforms using predicted contact to determine grasp and release commands while computing end effector action from the predicted object flow through optimization.

On the da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK), SurgFlow succeeds in 37 of 39 stage evaluations across tissue retraction, bimanual reveal, needle pickup, and needle handover, outperforming baselines trained on the same amount of data with or without action labels. The autonomous behavior transfers zero-shot to the new Humanoid-robot-based Laparoscopic Robot (LapSurgie) platform without adaptations, reaching average accuracies of 85% and 70% respectively in similar and completely novel view test cases.
