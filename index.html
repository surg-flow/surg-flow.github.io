Abstract

Manipulation intent has two parts: where to act on an object, and how that object should move. We represent both as 3D object-centric contact flow — a dense 3D flow field over object query points, paired with a probabilistic contact field defined on the same points. Unlike tool trajectories or robot joint actions, this pair is embodiment-invariant: it describes the task rather than the arm that performs it.

We extract contact flow from action-free surgical video using a training-free estimator that combines motion coupling, engagement gating and distance shading over tracked query points. It uses no force sensing and no proprioception, and is grounded per embodiment by two landmarks only — forceps jaws for a surgical robot, thumb and index finger for a human demonstrator.

A generator predicts future contact flow from a single point-cloud observation, and a model-based executor follows it: it grasps at the contact maximum and servos along the predicted flow with receding-horizon re-prediction, monitoring its own contact belief to detect slip and recover from it. We evaluate on a da Vinci Research Kit and on a humanoid laparoscopic platform, including transfer from human video alone.
